## Synthesis

### The central tension

**Open-source idealist × commercial operator.** The founder built credibility
by refusing monetization; the business now requires it. Left unspoken, this
reads as hypocrisy. Owned explicitly, it becomes the brand's most human proof
point: the person who said "no money" is the same person explaining why
quality needs revenue now.

### Resolution (how the tension is held, not eliminated)

- The MIT relicense already **transfers trust from license to transparency**:
  the code stays open even as the service becomes paid. Say this in public,
  once, clearly.
- The pivot announcement should be a **founder letter**, not marketing copy —
  voice M50 (candid, no drama): why CC BY-NC blocked the catalog, why MIT
  keeps the community whole.
- **Never pretend the past didn't exist.** The old README is in git history;
  the story must beat critics to it.

### Founder-led vs brand-led

- **Default: brand-led.** CineHD must outlive the handle — build product-brand
  assets (wordmark, voice, story) so the company isn't the GitHub profile.
- **Founder voice as amplifier, not foundation**: personal X/GitHub posts
  carry launch announcements and pivot letters; day-to-day product voice
  stays in the brand's first person ("on/nous", never "je" in product copy).
- **Bus-factor risk** is real and must be mitigated operationally (co-founder
  recruitment is part of the plan; multi-founder interview mode exists for
  this reason).
